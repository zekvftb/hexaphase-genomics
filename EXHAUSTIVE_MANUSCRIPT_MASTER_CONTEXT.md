# 1. SCIENTIFIC THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

### 1.1 The HexaPhase Concept
Standard genomics assumes a **1-dimensional, sequential paradigm**: 1 gene $\rightarrow$ 1 mRNA transcript $\rightarrow$ 1 protein. However, double-stranded DNA possesses **6 concurrent reading phases**:
* **Forward Strand:**
  * **Phase $+0$:** Triplet reading starting at nucleotide index $0$ ($\text{index} \equiv 0 \pmod 3$).
  * **Phase $+1$:** $+1$ base frameshift reading ($\text{index} \equiv 1 \pmod 3$).
  * **Phase $+2$:** $+2$ base frameshift reading ($\text{index} \equiv 2 \pmod 3$).
* **Antisense Reverse Strand:**
  * **Phase $-0$:** Reverse-complement reading phase 0.
  * **Phase $-1$:** Reverse-complement reading phase 1.
  * **Phase $-2$:** Reverse-complement reading phase 2.

### 3.1 Abstract
The displacement loop (D-Loop) of human mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) is universally classified in genomic databases as a non-coding regulatory region. Here, we demonstrate that coordinates `chrM:116..211 bp` (NCBI rCRS `NC_012920.1`) encode an evolutionary conserved 31-amino-acid signaling peptide (`MSQYLSLIPASSYYLSHLRSMLQANMLTKVC*` under vertebrate mitochondrial translation Table 2). These findings redefine the mitochondrial D-Loop as a coding locus and provide a novel biomarker and therapeutic target for oncology and mitochondrial medicine.

# 5. METHODS & MATHEMATICAL ALGORITHMS

### 5.1 HexaPhase Slicing Algorithm
For any nucleotide sequence $S = s_0 s_1 s_2 \dots s_{N-1}$:
$$\text{Phase } +k = \left( s_i \mid i \equiv k \pmod 3 \right), \quad k \in \{0, 1, 2\}$$
$$\text{Phase } -k = \left( \text{RC}(s)_i \mid i \equiv k \pmod 3 \right), \quad k \in \{0, 1, 2\}$$
where $\text{RC}(s)$ denotes the reverse-complement strand.

### 5.2 Kyte-Doolittle Mean Hydropathy
Given a peptide sequence $P = a_1 a_2 \dots a_L$ of length $L$:
$$\bar{H} = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{i=1}^{L} H(a_i)$$
where $H(a_i)$ is the Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy index score ($I=4.5, V=4.2, L=3.8, \dots, R=-4.5$).

### 5.3 Henderson-Hasselbalch Net Molecular Charge at pH 7.4
$$Q(\text{pH}) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{\text{pH} - \text{pK}_{N}}} - \frac{1}{1 + 10^{\text{pK}_{C} - \text{pH}}} + \sum_{b \in \{K, R, H\}} \frac{N_b}{1 + 10^{\text{pH} - \text{pK}_b}} - \sum_{a \in \{D, E, C, Y\}} \frac{N_a}{1 + 10^{\text{pK}_a - \text{pH}}}$$
where $\text{pK}_N = 9.6, \text{pK}_C = 2.3, \text{pK}_K = 10.5, \text{pK}_R = 12.5, \text{pK}_H = 6.0, \text{pK}_D = 3.9, \text{pK}_E = 4.3, \text{pK}_C = 8.3, \text{pK}_Y = 10.1$.

### 5.4 Amphipathic $\alpha$-Helical Hydrophobic Moment ($\mu_H$)
Calculated at standard $100^\circ$ angular pitch per residue for an $\alpha$-helix:
$$\mu_H = \frac{1}{L} \sqrt{\left[ \sum_{i=1}^L H(a_i) \sin(i \cdot 100^\circ) \right]^2 + \left[ \sum_{i=1}^L H(a_i) \cos(i \cdot 100^\circ) \right]^2}$$
